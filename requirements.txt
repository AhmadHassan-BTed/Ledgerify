Designing a robust React/TypeScript dashboard to parse and visualize Shopify Excel exports is an excellent project. Since you'll be hosting it on GitHub Pages, it will be a client-side application. You will likely use a library like `xlsx` (SheetJS) to parse the files locally in the user's browser.

Based on my analysis of your **"UK Orders.xlsx"** file, the most critical architectural decision you can make is to **ignore the "Summary" sheets entirely**. Summary sheets contain merged cells, blank rows, and formatting that break easily. **You should configure your software to only look for and parse the "Ledger" sheets** (e.g., `Ledger`, `Aug - Ledger`, etc.). The raw Ledger data contains everything you need to calculate your own summaries dynamically.

To ensure **robustness (so the app doesn't break if columns are rearranged)**, your TypeScript parser should map data using **Column Header Names** (e.g., `"Order Value"`, `"City"`) rather than column indexes (e.g., Column 8).

Here is a breakdown of the meaningful insights, functional requirements, and visualizations you should build for each specific team based on the data available in your export.

---

### 1. Finance Team

**Goal:** Track cash flow, revenue breakdowns, and financial liabilities.

* **Meaningful Insights:**
* **Total Revenue & AOV (Average Order Value):** Calculated by summing the `Order Value` column and dividing by the total number of orders.
* **Revenue by Product Category:** Sum of `Trio Amount`, `Discovery Amount`, `50ml Amount`, and `100ml Amount`.
* **Refund Liability:** Extracted from the `Status` column (e.g., refunded/partially refunded) to show lost revenue.


* **Visualizations:**
* **Top-level KPI Cards:** Total Revenue, AOV, Total Refunds.
* **Line Chart:** Daily or Monthly Revenue over time (using the `Date` and `Order Value` columns).
* **Stacked Bar Chart:** Revenue breakdown by product size (50ml vs 100ml vs Sets) per month.



### 2. CEO / Executives

**Goal:** High-level health of the business and quick growth metrics.

* **Meaningful Insights:**
* **Month-over-Month (MoM) Growth:** How the current month compares to the previous month in revenue and volume.
* **Customer Retention Rate:** Percentage of orders from returning customers (identifiable by counting duplicate `Email` addresses in the Ledger).
* **Top Market Penetration:** The best-performing countries or macro-regions.


* **Visualizations:**
* **Big Number KPI Cards (with trend indicators):** E.g., "£18,868 (+12% vs last month)".
* **Gauge Charts:** Tracking progress against a monthly revenue or order target.
* **Pie Chart:** New vs. Returning Customers.



### 3. Inventory Manager

**Goal:** Stock forecasting, product popularity, and supply chain management.

* **Meaningful Insights:**
* **Units Sold by Size:** Summing `Trio Qty`, `Discovery Qty`, `50ml Qty`, and `100ml Qty`.
* **Specific Fragrance Velocity:** You will need to write a script to parse the `Fragrance(s) Ordered` column (e.g., splitting "Visionary - 50ml; Silk Odyssey - 50ml" by the semicolon) to count exactly how many bottles of *Visionary* vs *Silk Odyssey* were sold.


* **Visualizations:**
* **Horizontal Bar Chart:** Top 10 selling individual fragrances.
* **Donut Chart:** Overall volume share (Sets vs 50ml vs 100ml).
* **Data Table:** A sortable table listing every specific fragrance, total units sold, and the date of the last sale to help with restocking orders.



### 4. Overviewer (Operations / General Manager)

**Goal:** Monitor daily operations, fulfillment bottlenecks, and logistics.

* **Meaningful Insights:**
* **Fulfillment Volume:** Number of orders placed per day of the week to staff the warehouse accordingly.
* **Geographic Shipping Hotspots:** Grouping by the `City` and `Province/State` columns (e.g., seeing that Rochdale or London have high volume).
* **Order Complexity:** The average `Order Quantity` per package (larger packages take longer to pack).


* **Visualizations:**
* **Heatmap / Calendar View:** Showing which days of the week get the most orders.
* **Map / Choropleth Chart:** A geographic map highlighting regions with the most orders (e.g., England vs. Scotland vs. Wales).
* **Data Table:** Unfulfilled, refunded, or pending orders needing attention.



### 5. Technical Person / Admin

**Goal:** Data integrity, bug tracking, and export health.

* **Meaningful Insights:**
* **Missing Data Checks:** Flagging orders with missing `Email`, missing `City`, or missing `Fragrance(s) Ordered`.
* **Anomaly Detection:** Highlighting orders with an `Order Value` of $0.00 or unusually high quantities (e.g., 50+ items).


* **Visualizations:**
* **Health Dashboard (Traffic Lights):** Green for clean data, Red if the uploaded file is missing required columns.
* **Sortable Table:** "Flagged Orders" containing specific Order #s (like #1782) that have missing fields so they can be fixed in Shopify.



### 6. Graphics / Creative Team

**Goal:** Knowing what to feature in visual ads and social media content.

* **Meaningful Insights:**
* **Hero Products:** The absolute best-selling scent (so they know which bottle to photograph).
* **Aesthetic Trends:** If "Trio Sets" are selling rapidly, they need to create more graphics featuring the Trio packaging.


* **Visualizations:**
* **Word Cloud:** Using the parsed names from `Fragrance(s) Ordered` (larger words = higher sales).
* **Image Grid / Big Cards:** Showcasing the Top 3 products of the month.



### 7. SEO & Marketing Team

**Goal:** Keyword targeting, email campaign segmentation, and geo-targeted ad spending.

* **Meaningful Insights:**
* **Geo-Targeting Data:** The `City` column shows exactly where demand is highest (e.g., 219 unique cities in the provided file). Marketing can run targeted Google/Facebook ads in those specific cities.
* **Bundle Behaviors / Cross-Selling:** Analyzing what fragrances are bought together. If people frequently buy "Supreme Power" and "Mirage" together, Marketing can create an email campaign bundling them.
* **Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) Indicators:** Frequency of returning customers by month.


* **Visualizations:**
* **Network Graph or Matrix:** Showing product correlations (which items are most frequently bought in the same cart).
* **Bar Chart:** Top 15 Cities by Revenue.
* **Cohort Table:** Showing how many users who bought in August returned in September.

### Designing the "Compare" Feature

To compare files (e.g., UK vs. USA, or Q1 vs Q2), your React state should accept an array of uploaded files.

* **UI Layout:** Create a split-screen or toggle-based UI.
* **Delta Metrics:** Whenever comparing, the software should automatically calculate the percentage difference for key metrics. If the UK file has an AOV of £35 and the USA file has an AOV of $50, show the raw numbers alongside a `+X%` badge.
* **Overlay Charts:** Use Multi-line charts (e.g., Chart.js or Recharts) where Line 1 is Dataset A (UK) and Line 2 is Dataset B (USA) plotted across the same X-axis (days 1-30 of the month).

### Summary of Tech Stack Recommendations:

* **Framework:** React (Next.js or Vite) + TypeScript.
* **Hosting:** GitHub Pages (requires standard static export).
* **Excel Parsing:** `xlsx` (SheetJS) — specifically reading the `Ledger` sheet and converting it to JSON using `XLSX.utils.sheet_to_json`. This automatically maps column headers to JSON keys, fulfilling your robustness requirement.
* **Visualizations:** `Recharts` or `Chart.js` (both are excellent, highly customizable, and responsive for React).


To make this dashboard fast, crash-proof, and capable of analyzing multiple large Shopify exports directly in the browser on GitHub Pages, here is the best modern tech stack to build it.

---

### 1. Core Framework & Build Tool

* **Vite + React (TypeScript):** Vite offers instant HMR and optimized static production builds (`vite build`), which export straight into a `/dist` folder ready for GitHub Pages.
* **TypeScript (Strict Mode):** Essential for defining data contracts across messy spreadsheets so mismatched types don't cause runtime errors.

### 2. High-Performance Data Engine (The Game Changer)

Standard JavaScript loops (`.filter()`, `.reduce()`) choke when crunching tens of thousands of rows across multiple files and dates.

* **DuckDB-Wasm (In-Browser SQL Engine):**
* **Why it makes you powerful:** DuckDB runs directly inside the browser using WebAssembly. You can load your parsed Excel sheets into DuckDB tables and execute analytical SQL queries (`GROUP BY`, `SUM`, `JOIN`, Window functions) across multiple files in milliseconds.
* **Compare Feature:** Joining the UK dataset and US dataset via SQL (`FULL OUTER JOIN` or `UNION ALL`) becomes trivial and instantaneous.


* **Web Workers:** Run the Excel parsing and heavy metric calculations in a background thread so the user interface never freezes when someone drops in 5 workbooks at once.

### 3. Crash-Proof File Ingestion & Parsing

Spreadsheets frequently have rearranged columns, misspelled headers, or missing cells.

* **SheetJS (`xlsx`):** The industry standard for reading `.xlsx` files into raw JSON rows in memory.
* **Zod (Schema Validation & Normalization):**
* Create a resilient schema adapter. If Shopify names a column `"Order Value"` in one export, `"Total"` in another, or moves it from column 8 to column 2, a Zod pre-processor maps them to a normalized interface:
```ts
// Example normalizer mapping
const normalizeHeader = (key: string) => key.trim().toLowerCase().replace(/[^a-z0-9]/g, '');

```


* Missing fields get populated with sensible defaults (`0`, `""`, or `null`) instead of crashing the app with `Cannot read property of undefined`.



### 4. Client-Side Storage (Zero Backend Needed)

* **Dexie.js (IndexedDB Wrapper):**
* When a user uploads their UK and US workbooks, save the parsed datasets into the browser’s **IndexedDB**.
* When the user reloads or navigates away, the data remains instantly accessible without requiring them to re-upload the files each time.



### 5. UI, Tables & Visualizations

* **UI Component Layer:** **Tailwind CSS + shadcn/ui (Radix Primitives)**
* Gives you accessible tabs (one tab for Finance, one for CEO, etc.), modals, date pickers, dropdowns, and dark mode out of the box.


* **Data Grid:** **TanStack Table v8 (React Table) + TanStack Virtual**
* Headless table architecture with column sorting, fuzzy search, multi-column filtering, and CSV export.
* Virtualized rendering ensures that a table with 10,000+ orders renders only the visible rows, maintaining 60 FPS scrolling.


* **Charts & Analytics:** **Apache ECharts (`echarts-for-react`)**
* While Recharts is common, **ECharts** is far more capable for an executive dashboard:
* Handles hundreds of thousands of data points without dropping frames.
* Built-in support for **heatmaps** (order volume by day/hour), **geo-maps/choropleths** (UK/US sales by region), **donut charts**, and **multi-series comparative lines** with zoom sliders.

### Recommended Project Architecture

```text
src/
├── app/                  # Layout, multi-tab routing (Finance, CEO, Inventory, etc.)
├── components/
│   ├── charts/           # ECharts wrappers (RevenueChart, GeoMap, Heatmap)
│   ├── tables/           # TanStack virtualized order grids
│   └── ui/               # shadcn/ui components (Tabs, Cards, Dropdowns)
├── core/
│   ├── parser/           # SheetJS worker + Zod schema sanitizers
│   ├── db/               # DuckDB-Wasm / Dexie.js storage & queries
│   └── analytics/        # Business logic calculators (AOV, MoM, Bundles)
└── types/                # Normalized Order, Customer, and Fragrance types

```

### Deployment to GitHub Pages

Set up a simple GitHub Actions workflow (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`):

```yaml
name: Deploy Dashboard
on:
  push:
    branches: [main]
jobs:
  build-and-deploy:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v4
      - uses: actions/setup-node@v4
        with:
          node-version: 20
      - run: npm ci
      - run: npm run build
      - uses: actions/upload-pages-artifact@v3
        with:
          path: ./dist
      - uses: actions/deploy-pages@v4

```
